Where the Commandment Lands

Jesus did to the law what AI governance still has to do to oversight: he moved the question from the visible act to the formation that precedes it.

Ten commandments. Two great commands that sum them. Four pillars that carry them into an age of intelligent systems. This page is the connective tissue — and the reason a compliance-layer reading of Scripture cannot produce literacy about tools that do their work upstream of choice.

The same defense, two thousand years apart

The compliance-layer defense

"I did not murder. I did not commit adultery."

The act is clean. The record shows nothing. And Jesus answers that the anger was already the thing, the look was already the thing — the commandment was never aiming at the hand alone.

Its modern twin

"A human reviewed it. A person clicked approve."

The record shows a human decision. But the click happened inside a space the system had already shaped — options generated, ranked, filtered. The person chose among survivors. That is a selection, not a decision.

Both defenses are true at the layer they name. Both answer the wrong question. Sin and software do their most consequential work before the moment anyone would call a choice — which is why the law, read the way Jesus reads it, is already calibrated to this problem. It is not a devotional overlay on a technology conversation. It is the older, more precise account of how formation precedes act.

Four texts hold this up

If any link is weak, the rest is decoration. Check them.

i

The two commands sum the law

Jesus states the summation himself: the whole law hangs on loving God and loving neighbor.

Matthew 22:37–40
ii

The second table is neighbor-love

Paul does the arithmetic: adultery, murder, theft, coveting — summed up in one word.

Romans 13:8–10 · James 2:8
iii

The commandments address the heart

Jesus lists commandments six through nine and locates their origin in the interior.

Matthew 15:19 · Mark 7:21–23
iv

The tenth was always interior

Coveting has no external act. Jesus is not adding interiority — he reads the nine the way the tenth was always written.

Romans 7:7 · Luke 12:15

The First Table

Know God · Love God

Four commandments about God — which turn out to govern what you consult first, what you reduce Him to, whose authority your words borrow, and who owns your output.

1

No other gods before me

Exodus 20:3
Where it landsStated

"You cannot serve God and money."

Matthew 6:24 · "Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also," 6:21

What it governs

Not chiefly statues — functional ultimacy. What you consult, trust, and rely on before anything else.

When you have a question, what do you turn to first?

2

No graven image

Exodus 20:4–6
Where it landsImplied

"God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth."

John 4:24

What it governs

Reducing God to something we fashion and can manage. The image is safer than the God — that is the appeal, and the violation.

Are you relating to God, or to a representation of God that you made and can control?

3

Not taking the name in vain

Exodus 20:7
Where it landsStated

"Let what you say be simply 'Yes' or 'No.'"

Matthew 5:33–37

What it governs

Invoking a name to lend weight to words. Jesus removes the machinery of oath entirely: your word carries only what you actually stand behind.

Does your speech carry authority you have not earned?

4

Remember the Sabbath

Exodus 20:8–11 · Deuteronomy 5:12–15
Where it landsStated

"The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath."

Mark 2:27

What it governs

Deuteronomy grounds it in deliverance: remember you were a slave. Rest is the weekly declaration that a people is no longer owned by its output.

In an economy organized around optimizing everything — who owns your output?

The Hinge

Love God · Love People

The commandment that faces both directions — and the sharpest text in this framework for anyone who governs systems.

5

Honor your father and mother

Exodus 20:12
Where it landsStated

"You void the word of God by your tradition."

Mark 7:9–13 — the Corban ruling: a procedurally valid dedication that released a man from supporting his parents

What it governs

Honor runs backward to those who formed you and forward to those you are forming. Jesus does not call Corban a loophole. He calls it voiding the word of God.

Does your technical compliance void the actual obligation?

The Second Table

Love People · Live Faithfully

Five commandments about the neighbor — each one, in Jesus's reading, already violated in the interior before anything visible happens.

6

You shall not murder

Exodus 20:13
Where it landsStated

"Everyone who is angry with his brother will be liable to judgment… whoever says, 'You fool!' will be liable."

Matthew 5:21–22 · "Everyone who hates his brother is a murderer," 1 John 3:15

What it governs

Contempt — the reduction of a person to something less than a person. Jesus locates murder's root there.

Where does contempt become procedure? Scored, sorted, and treated as the score — contempt at scale does not feel like contempt. It feels like efficiency.

7

You shall not commit adultery

Exodus 20:14
Where it landsStated

"Everyone who looks at a woman with lustful intent has already committed adultery with her in his heart."

Matthew 5:27–28

What it governs

A private act, no external partner, no visible victim — and Jesus says the violation is complete. The interiorization that maps onto synthetic intimacy with uncomfortable precision.

Is fidelity intact where no one can see and no one appears to be harmed?

Lands in → Machine Companions & Human Presence, extended to synthetic intimacy
8

You shall not steal

Exodus 20:15
Where it landsStatedApplied

"Out of the heart come… thefts."

Matthew 15:19 — heart-origin stated; the data extension is application

What it governs

Taking what is not yours — including from people who never agreed and often never knew.

Is this mine to give, or mine to take? The rule for AI inputs — is this mine to share? — is a theft question before it is a privacy question.

Lands in → Sacred Boundaries & Confidences · The Person Behind the Data
9

You shall not bear false witness

Exodus 20:16
Where it landsStated

"Out of the heart come… false witness."

Matthew 15:19 · "Let your yes be yes," Matthew 5:37

What it governs

Note what it protects: a neighbor, not an abstraction. False witness harms a specific person — which makes verification an act of love, not pedantry.

Whose word does it become when you repeat it? Unverified output passed along is false witness borne, whether or not you intended to deceive.

Lands in → Truth & Verification · Synthetic Media & False Witness
10

You shall not covet

Exodus 20:17
Where it landsStated

"Take care, and be on your guard against all covetousness."

Luke 12:15 · "I would not have known what it is to covet if the law had not said…," Romans 7:7

What it governs

The commandment with no external act — and therefore the key to all the others. Wanting itself is under command.

Who is shaping what you want? Ranking and recommendation are not neutral about desire. Shaping wanting is the business model.
